The story behind the song

It started as a drawing.

Nobody can tell you how hard it is to get better. The veteran this song came from had been trying to explain it to the people treating him, and the words kept failing. So he drew it instead. One page: a figure at the bottom, a path going up, and lines across the path.

Every line is a wall of glass. The thin ones break with a push. The thick ones don't break at all. None of them can be seen. You find out which kind you've hit by hitting it, and the only thing you have to swing is what you were given.

The X's at the bottom are where you start, and where you land when a wall doesn't give. Look at the sign he planted there. He wrote "safe zone" first, then wrote over it: alive but not living zone. It isn't safe. It's quiet. Nothing hurts down there and nothing moves. You can breathe, and that's about all you can do. The song's name for it became the hook.

I don't live there.I only breathe there.So I'm walking back in.

from I Can't See the Glass

The further up the path, the thicker the glass, and a wall that holds doesn't just stop you. It throws you all the way back down, and the fall is worse than the wall. Everyone's walls are a different thickness. Nobody can tell you yours.

At the top of the page he drew a smiley face and wrote next to it: treated, all better, life great. He wrote it the way you'd write a dare.

What the glass is

He asked for the bridge to say it plainly, so it does. The glass is everything that happened, and everything he saw that he can't put down. Get through one wall and there may be another behind it. Or there may not. He asked. Nobody could tell him.

Every wall's a different thickness,and nobody can tell you that.

from I Can't See the Glass

Why he walks anyway

He doesn't know what's at the top. Maybe it's more glass, maybe it's not. Something in him won't stay down, and in the song it's the same thing that brought him home. So the last line isn't a promise. It's a decision.

I don't know if it's better.I'm walking anyway.

from I Can't See the Glass
