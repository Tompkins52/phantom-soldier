The story behind the song

Somebody asked him what he'd tell a kid about enlisting.

A kid at work, half his age, asked what advice he'd give someone who wanted to sign up. The answer took half an hour, and by the end of it the kid was looking at him like the question had been a lot bigger than it sounded.

Why did he join? He was seventeen. He thought it would be cool, and that girls liked a uniform. He owns that without flinching, and so does the song. It opens in a folding chair in a strip-mall office, with a government pen.

Would he do it again? In a heartbeat. Not for the same reasons. He stayed for the ones he found: lessons you can't learn anywhere else, and people who could call him twenty years from now, with nothing in between, and he'd already be reaching for his keys. All they'd have to say is where and when.

You'll join for all the wrong reasonsand stay for the ones you find.

The part that isn't in the brochure

What you do stays done, right or wrong, and it walks with you for the rest of your life. You can't undo the past. And sometimes doing the right thing is the most difficult thing you will ever have to do. He told the kid all of that too, because leaving it out would have been a lie.

The long answer

So the song is the whole answer, aimed at the kid in the folding chair, who is also the man he used to be. The back half drops to one voice and no instruments, because the last thing he'd say to that kid deserves nothing behind it.
