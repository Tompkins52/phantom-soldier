The story behind the song

It started with three words. I want to want.

There's a stretch of getting better that nobody puts on a poster. You know every reason to keep going. You could say them in your sleep. You just can't feel any of them yet. Wanting to want is the step before hope, and it's a real step. It counts. That's the whole song.

Nobody in it gets fixed. He gets up because the dogs need out. He waters a patch by the fence where nothing has come up. He gets through the day, one thing and then the next, and some days he picks up the phone. None of that is the feeling. It's what you do while you wait for it.

I want to want the morning.I want to want my life.

from I Want to Want

The seed you can't dig up

You can't dig up a seed to see how it's doing. You water the dirt and you wait. From the outside, most of getting better looks exactly like that: nothing, then more nothing, and somebody still out there every morning anyway. The bridge is the first morning he catches himself bending down to look for green. Nothing's there yet. But he looked, and looking is a want.

Nothing yet. But I looked.I wanted it to be there.

from I Want to Want

The dogs

The song starts with the dogs and ends with them, because they were there the whole way. They don't need the night explained. They lie close through it, keep watch, and ask for nothing but the door in the morning. For a lot of people that's what gets them up before anything else does, and that's not a small thing.

Tonight the dogs are pressed against my side.They'll keep watch like they always do.

from I Want to Want

The verses are one voice, because they're a story. The chorus gets company, because that part belongs to anybody who's been there and wants to sing it back.
